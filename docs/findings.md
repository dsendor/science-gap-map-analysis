## What this shows

### The maturity gradient did not replicate

An earlier version of this document reported a monotone gradient — the share of each
capability type already working now running smoothly from LLM reasoning at the top to
physical build at zero — and read it as the verifier's-law prediction confirmed on
someone else's data. **A full independent relabel of all 103 gaps did not reproduce it.**

| Primary AI type | v1 working-now | v2 working-now |
|---|---:|---:|
| Coordination and institutional | 7% | 67% |
| LLM reasoning and synthesis | 60% | 60% |
| Autonomous experimentation | 40% | 56% |
| Sensing and signal processing | 20% | 53% |
| Real-time control of physical systems | n/a | 50% |
| ML surrogates and prediction | 30% | 14% |
| Design and optimization search | 19% | 13% |
| Physical build and manipulation | 0% | 13% |

The ordering inverts at the top. Coordination and institutional moves from last to first.
Physical build stops being zero. Maturity agreement between the two passes was 63 of 103,
and 23 of the 40 disagreements moved the same direction, from `2-5 years` to `Working now`.

The cause is a definitional ambiguity the earlier revision did not close: does `Working
now` mean the capability **exists** or that applying it **would move this gap**? For
technical categories the readings coincide. For institutional ones they do not — convening
a standards body is available today; getting universal DNA-synthesis screening adopted is
not. See `methodology/taxonomy.md` ambiguity (b).

**Consequence: the verifiability gradient is withdrawn as a finding.** It was the most
striking thing this analysis produced and it does not survive independent replication.
Maturity is reported per gap with its disagreement rate attached, and no aggregate claim
rests on it.

### What does survive

**The type distribution is the stable result.** Primary AI type agreed on 77 of 103 gaps
between two independent passes (25% disagreement), against 61% agreement on maturity.
Two figures held almost exactly across both passes:

- **LLM reasoning and synthesis is primary for 10 of 103 gaps** — identical in v1 and v2 — and 60% working-now in both. The cognitive layer is a small and well-characterised slice of this map.
- Coordination and institutional is primary for 15 of 103 in both passes. The two passes disagree sharply about how *mature* institutional capability is, and not at all about how *often* it is the primary constraint.

So the defensible version of the claim is the weaker one: **LLM-shaped work is primary
for about a tenth of the map, and the rest is spread across sensing, prediction, design,
build and institutions.** The stronger claim — that AI maturity tracks verifiability in a
clean ordering — is not supported by this data at this level of measurement reliability.

### A category that does not work

The audit found that **Proxy only cannot be applied reliably.** It ran 78% disagreement against 0% for Directly measurable and 9% for Verification contested, and all three auditors independently reported it was repeatedly the nearest alternative and almost never won. Every Proxy only assignment has therefore been downgraded to `guess`, including the ones the auditor did not sample.

This matters for reading everything above: the tiers that carry the argument are the two that held up. Tier 1 and tier 3 are reliable; the residual category between them is not, and a future version should either define it more sharply or drop it.

### Taxonomy gaps found by audit

Three cases where the seven-type taxonomy has no right answer, all surfaced independently by blind auditors:

- **Closed-loop control of a physical system** has no home. Fusion plasma control chooses no experiments, so it is not autonomous experimentation, and it builds nothing, so it is not physical build. This is a defensible eighth category.
- **Gaps where AI is the object rather than the instrument** (AI misuse, rogue AI, narrow reasoning) have no safety-and-robustness-research bucket.
- **Composite gaps** bundle sub-problems that would take different types *and different tiers*, so a single primary loses real information.
